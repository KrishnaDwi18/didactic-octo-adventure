# Original academic expression examples

These are newly written illustrative expressions, not a transcription of either uploaded book. Adapt to evidence and context.

## Establishing context
- The significance of this question becomes clearer when viewed alongside ...
- This debate connects domestic policy choices with wider international constraints.

## Identifying a research gap
- An unresolved issue concerns how ...
- Less clear is the mechanism linking ... to ...

## Comparing scholarship
- These accounts differ primarily in their explanation of ...
- Whereas one approach emphasizes ..., another foregrounds ...

## Developing a theoretical argument
- The analytical value of this concept lies in its ability to distinguish ... from ...
- This framework directs attention to the relationship between ... and ...

## Describing methods
- The cases were selected to examine variation in ...
- The analysis combines ... with ... to trace ...

## Interpreting findings
- The available evidence is consistent with ..., although it does not establish ...
- An alternative interpretation is that ...

## Drawing conclusions
- The analysis points to a conditional relationship between ... and ...
- These findings invite further investigation of ...

## Usage constraints
Never claim a research gap, trend, causal link, or finding without supporting evidence. Do not add named scholars or citations unless provided or verified.

## Academic Vocabulary and Sentence Patterns

### 1. Introducing an Argument
- This study examines the relationship between X and Y.
- The central argument advanced here is that...
- An important consideration in this debate concerns...

### 2. Expressing Relationships
- X is closely associated with Y.
- The relationship between X and Y is shaped by...
- X may influence Y under particular institutional conditions.

### 3. Introducing Contrasts
- While X may contribute to Y, its effects depend on...
- However, this interpretation does not fully account for...
- In contrast, an alternative perspective emphasizes...

### 4. Expressing Caution
- The available evidence suggests that...
- This relationship should not be interpreted as necessarily causal.
- The extent to which X contributes to Y remains uncertain.

### 5. Explaining Implications
- These considerations have implications for...
- This raises questions concerning...
- Taken together, these observations suggest that...

### 6. Vocabulary for Social Sciences and International Relations
- resource governance
- domestic value addition
- institutional capacity
- regulatory effectiveness
- political economy
- interstate relations
- distributional outcomes
- policy implementation
- structural constraints
- international cooperation

### Usage Rules
- Use phrases only when they match the author's intended meaning.
- Replace X and Y with specific concepts.
- Do not add causal relationships without supporting evidence.
- Avoid using multiple formulaic expressions in one paragraph.
- Prefer clear sentences over unnecessarily complex academic language.
